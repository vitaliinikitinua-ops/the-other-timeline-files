# EP11 / Video #1 — BRIEF: Buy Now, Pay Later on Black Friday
Viewer: young worker 22-30 (first job, first card, lives paycheck to paycheck, shops on the phone).
Goal: after watching, the viewer can turn "4 easy payments of $50" into the REAL price (full sum, hours of work, cost if it goes wrong).
Tone: irony + drama. End date for all data: Sep 2026 (APR = Aug 2026, latest FRED value).

## Problem (viewer's words)
"It's only $50 every two weeks... why is my whole January paycheck already gone?"

## Causes
1. Price splitting: the brain compares $50 with the wallet, not $200 with the budget ("pain of paying" is cut by 4).
2. Stacking: every plan looks small alone; nobody sees the total. CFPB (Jan 2025): 63% of BNPL borrowers had several loans at once, 33% across different apps; peaks in the holiday season.
3. Spending lift: BNPL is paid by merchants because people buy more. NBER w30508 (Di Maggio, Katz, Williams 2022): first BNPL use -> lasting rise in spending, ~$60/week (press summary; re-check paper).
4. Invisible debt: BNPL usually not reported to credit bureaus -> no one, including you, sees the full picture.
5. Black Friday pressure: timers, "only today", checkout buttons. Adobe: $20.0B spent via BNPL in holiday 2025 (+9.8%), Cyber Monday alone $1.03B.

## Cost (real numbers)
- Late payments are common: LendingTree 2025 — 41% of BNPL users paid late in the past year (34% a year before); 25% used BNPL for groceries.
- Late fees: Klarna up to $7, Afterpay up to $8 (cap 25% of order); Affirm no late fees (interest on longer plans instead).
- When BNPL breaks, the gap goes to the credit card: avg APR on accounts paying interest = 21.19% (FRED TERMCBCCALLNS, Aug 2026).
- EXAMPLE: $800 of "extra" Black Friday stuff on a card, minimum payments (1% + interest, min $25) -> 48 months, $389 interest, $1,189 total (computed).
- EXAMPLE: same $800 in S&P 500 (price index only, no dividends) Sep 2016 -> Sep 2026 = x3.55 -> ~$2,844 (sp500.csv).
- Hours of life: avg hourly wage $38.00 (FRED CES0500000003, Oct 2026) -> $1,200 cart = 31.6 hours = almost 4 work days.

## Myths
- "It's interest-free, so it's free" — free only if every payment is on time and you would have bought it anyway.
- "It's not real debt" — it is a loan; missed payments can hit your credit (some providers now report).
- "I'm saving 40% on Black Friday" — 40% off something you didn't plan = 60% spent.

## Solutions (honest limits)
1. Full-price test: before "pay in 4", say the full sum out loud — would I pay it in cash today? (simple, doesn't fix overspending alone)
2. Hours test: price / your hourly pay after tax.
3. One-plan rule: max ONE active BNPL plan at a time; never for food or bills.
4. 48-hour cart: leave it in the cart for 2 days; Black Friday "deals" mostly come back on Cyber Monday / Christmas sales.
5. If already stacked: list all plans with dates in one calendar, turn on autopay from a day after payday, pay the smallest first; don't fill gaps with a card.
No provider or broker recommendations. Not financial advice.
